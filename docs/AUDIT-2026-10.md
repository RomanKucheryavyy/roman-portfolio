# Consistency audit — October 2026

Eight parallel lenses over the codebase plus a synthesis and an adversarial
critic. ~1.3M tokens of analysis. Every claim below cites a real file:line —
verify before acting, the code moves.

## Diagnosis

Your site isn't glitchy because of bad taste — it's glitchy because the same nine projects are drawn by six hand-written card layouts that were never reconciled, and nothing in the codebase forces them to agree. There is no shared card, no shared section shell, no type scale, and a 39-token design system in globals.css (lines 45–85) that literally zero components reference (verified: `grep "var(--card-|var(--nav-|var(--btn-|var(--terminal-|var(--cert-" src/ --include=*.tsx` returns nothing). So every surface improvised, and the improvisations drifted. The result is exactly what you described: your phone shows Alongside Coffee cropped to the middle 47% of the screenshot (`Projects.tsx:307` aspect-[3/4] against a 1280×800 source — I measured all nine PNGs, every one is 1.600), two taps later `/works` shows the same file uncropped at 16/10, the desktop deck colour-grades each card with its brand hex while the mobile deck silently drops the tint, and clicking a card opens a modal on the home page but throws you off-site on /works. The "deep dive" modal is the worst offender: it has no link, no status, no colour (confirmed — `grep "url|status|isApp|color" ProjectModal.tsx` returns nothing), blurs the screenshot to wallpaper, and prints a hardcoded "Role: Design & Development" for all nine pieces including the Swift app. Underneath that sit real but second-order problems — six headings on four different left edges, a content column that gets *narrower* at 768px, `--radius-2xl: 1rem` being smaller than `--radius-xl: 24px`, 24 font sizes from five sizing systems, and `/works` and `/resume` inheriting `* { cursor: none }` from globals.css:88-90 while never mounting the Cursor component, so you hover those pages with no pointer at all. Fix the card first. Almost everything a visitor calls "random" is downstream of that one missing component.

## Root causes

- No shared card component: PROJECTS[] is rendered by six independent implementations — DesktopDeck (Projects.tsx:147-237), MobileDeck (Projects.tsx:306-382), ProjectModal.tsx:75-146, works-wheel.tsx:321-376, works/page.tsx:69-108, and Compositions.tsx:18-73 — agreeing on nothing: 4 image crops, 4 radii, 5 tag-pill variants, 5 link affordances, 4 treatments of unlaunched work.
- No shared section shell: all seven sections re-declare padding, container and inset from scratch (Hero.tsx:61, Measures.tsx:97, Projects.tsx:434, Compositions.tsx:111, Manifesto.tsx:35, Conductor.tsx:119, Compose.tsx:184), so they drifted into py-14 vs py-16, max-w-6xl vs max-w-4xl vs none, and the stepped px-6 md:px-16 that makes the column shrink 79px when the viewport grows 1px.
- A design-token layer that nothing obeys: globals.css:45-85 defines 39 semantic tokens with zero .tsx consumers — proven by globals.css:78-80 defining --terminal-dot-red/-yellow/-green while CodePeek.tsx:153-155, Compose.tsx:219-222 and Conductor.tsx:182-184 all hardcode #ff5f57/#febc2e/#28c840. With nothing enforced, components reach for raw Tailwind opacities and 13 text values appear for ~5 roles.
- No type scale: @theme (globals.css:4-42) declares families, colours and radii but not one font-size token, so 24 distinct sizes arrive from five systems (Tailwind tokens, arbitrary px, rem, vw, JS-computed), and 91 of 201 size declarations land under 12px.
- Five disagreeing definitions of 'mobile': useDeviceCapability.ts:27 (pointer:coarse OR width<768, read once in useState and never resubscribed), useMediaQuery('(max-width: 767px), (pointer: coarse)') in AmbientBackground/DNAHelix, useMediaQuery('(max-width: 768px)') in MobileReveal/SectionFade, ScatterText.tsx:80's own inline check, and width-only Tailwind md:. Tablets and resized windows therefore get an incoherent mix of the two designs.
- Route chrome lives in page.tsx instead of layout.tsx: Nav, Cursor, AmbientBackground and the noise overlay are mounted only at page.tsx:77-82, while layout.tsx:36-45 mounts none of them — so /works and /resume are a different website that has lost the pointer.
- Features shipped and then half-abandoned: SectionFade's `transition` prop is typed, documented, passed six distinct values in page.tsx:87-107 and never read (SectionFade.tsx:16 destructures only children and className); BeatPattern.tsx is 117 lines imported by nothing; four of CodePeek's six snippets name files that do not exist in the repo (verified by find).

## Per-lens summaries

### Presentation consistency — how the same piece of work is rendered across every surface (PROJECTS[] in 5 UIs, COMPOSITIONS[] in a 6th)

The owner's complaint is accurate and the lens confirms the mechanism: nine projects are rendered by six independently hand-written card layouts that agree on almost nothing — four image crops of the same 1280×800 screenshots, four corner radii, five tag-pill variants, five ways to offer the live link and four ways to present unlaunched work — and the "deep dive" modal is the weakest of the six, dropping the link, the status, the brand color and the legible screenshot while inventing a hardcoded "Role" line. The split between PROJECTS and COMPOSITIONS is substantively real (client work vs. owned products) but is communicated only by styling it differently, which reads as randomness rather than as a distinction.

_14 findings; 6 high severity._

### typography

Bad — this is the dimension that most directly explains the owner's complaint. There is no type scale at all: 24 distinct font sizes drawn from five incompatible sizing systems (Tailwind tokens, arbitrary px, rem, vw, clamp, and JS-computed px), with 91 of 201 size declarations (45%) falling below 12px — including the contact email at 9px/10% opacity and timeline labels at 9px/25%. The vertical padding is actually 5-of-7 consistent, so the vertical rhythm is close to fine — it is the horizontal grid and the hand-tuned per-section margins (5 card paddings, 3 grid gaps, 4 heading-gap values) that read as unfinished, and all of it traces to there being no shared section shell anywhere in the codebase.

_12 findings; 4 high severity._

### Colour and surface treatment

The owner's complaint is accurate and the cause is specific: a 40-declaration semantic token layer in globals.css has zero consumers, so every component improvises, producing 13 distinct text opacities, 8 border values and 5 surface fills for about 12 real roles — including the same tag pill at five different colour pairs and the same project description visibly brightening from white/50 to white/60 when its modal opens. _7 findings; 4 high severity._

### Motion and animation vocabulary

The owner's complaint is accurate and the cause is mechanical: five animation systems (GSAP+ScrollTrigger, framer-motion, raw CSS keyframes, Lenis, and nine hand-rolled rAF loops) each own a piece of the same job, and nothing arbitrates between them. "Reveal on scroll" alone exists five times with seven durations, five easings, and entry thresholds spread from `top 65%` to `top 88%` — and on phones three of those five fire on the *same* element at once, so a card fades, then lifts, then fades again. Two elements have two systems writing the same CSS property every frame, which is the literal glitchiness: the hero's scroll fade desyncs from its scroll movement by half a second, and the hero's gyro loop overwrites the hero's scroll parallax entirely on any touch-capable machine. Reduced motion is checked in 11 places and ignored in roughly 20, including Lenis itself and the whole 2.8-second boot loader.

_18 findings; 6 high severity._

### Copy, labels and naming

The owner's complaint is literally true at the copy layer: one collection of nine client projects is called six different names across six files, the two project sections both have "Work(s)" in their labels while the sentence that would distinguish them exists only in a code comment, and the nav presents five metaphor words that name none of the four things a recruiter is scanning for.
